VeritasRAG Clinical Pharmacology Reference: Ibuprofen

Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) derived from propionic acid.
It was discovered by Stewart Adams and John Nicholson in 1961 at the research department of the Boots Group in the United Kingdom.
The standard adult oral dosage for mild to moderate pain is 200 mg to 400 mg every 4 to 6 hours as needed.
The maximum recommended over-the-counter daily dose should not exceed 1200 mg in 24 hours unless directed by a licensed physician.
Prescription-strength formulations may reach up to 3200 mg daily divided into multiple doses under strict clinical supervision.
Common indications include headache, dental pain, primary dysmenorrhea, muscular aches, and fever reduction.
Possible adverse reactions include gastrointestinal distress, dyspepsia, and increased cardiovascular risks with prolonged high-dose usage.
